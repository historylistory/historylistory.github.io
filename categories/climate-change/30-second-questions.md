One (1) Senator responded with the following on July 23, 2026: As you know permitting reform is needed to expedite the process for states and private entities to develop American infrastructure.  As a member of the Senate Committee on Environment and Public Works (EPW), I am a cosponsor of the Spur Permitting of Underdeveloped Resources Act (SPUR) Act, Accelerating Deployment of Versatile, Advanced Nuclear for Clean Energy (ADVANCE) Act, and Revitalizing the Economy by Simplifying Timelines and Assuring Regulatory Transparency (RESTART) Act to reform the permitting and environmental review processes. Both the SPUR and RESTART Acts would expedite the permitting process and expand energy production, therefore reducing energy prices.  Both pieces of legislation would streamline the agency review process with enforceable timelines, enact time limits to prevent countless legal challenges and modernize current laws while preserving environmental protections.  Improvements to the federal permitting process will strengthen the development of our power generation, transmission, infrastructure, and flood control. The ADVANCE Act supports the adoption of nuclear energy technology to ensure American energy independence and security.  I'm proud to support this effort to facilitate America's nuclear leadership, modernize current technology, and improve the environment.  This legislation will benefit operating reactors, like (STATE) own (CITY) Nuclear Station, and assist with decommissioning of reactors, like (CITY). In total, these pieces of legislation will reform federal energy permitting to improve our energy and the American economy.  Making government work better for taxpayers was one of the things I was most proud of when I was Governor, and it continues to be one of my main priorities in the U.S. Senate. My primary mission as your United States Senator is to make government work with proven (STATE) solutions that are ready for America.  To better inform my decision-making, it is important that I hear the ideas, concerns, and opinions of all (STATE).  I appreciate the time and effort you took to provide input on clean energy permitting reform and will keep it in mind as the U.S. Senate considers this issue.

A second (2) Congerssman responded on July 29, 2026 with the following: Thank you for contacting our congressional offices with your views and concerns. I appreciate the opportunity to engage with you on the matter of protecting the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness and its surrounding nature. Listed within H.R. 9171—Department of the Interior Environment, and Related Agencies Appropriations Act of 2027, Section 441 suggests a relaxed change to the mining prohibitions on the Boundary Waters region. The overall bill itself carries a large variety of environmental policy issues to be considered, and when making legislative deliberations, I believe that concerns regarding balancing wildlife protection and economic pursuits ought to be recognized in the process. As Congress moves forward with debating this legislation, I will be sure to keep your thoughts in mind. Nevertheless, in line with my beliefs on thoughtful lawmaking, earlier this year I voted against H.J.Res.140, relating to the nullification of Public Land Order No.7917 and the same associated Boundary Waters area. The challenging decision came after thorough discussions with approximately 30 members of our (STATE) community, who frequent the waters and are property owners in the area. The constituents expressed concerns regarding the potential for contamination of their nearby drinking water, which is sourced from the unfiltered lakes, upon the sulfide-ore mining and subsequent acid mine drainage in the watershed. From our conversation, I could tell that they feared the impact that mining would have on their limited water source and their concerns led me to err on the side of caution. Although the Boundary Waters Canoe Area Wilderness possesses immense capacity to strengthen our domestic mineral production and economy, I found that we must first maintain a balanced approach in our economic endeavors, especially in the face of potential public health risks. In 2023, the Biden administration passed Public Land Order (PLO) No. 7917, withdrawing nearly 225,504 acres of National Forest System lands in Cook, Lake, and Saint Louis counties, Minnesota, from disposition under the United States mineral and geothermal leasing laws, for a period of 20 years. The intended purpose of the PLO is to protect and preserve the ecosystems and the reserves of natural resources within the area from the effects of exploration, extraction, and exhaustion. The subsequent H.J.Res.140, of 2026, then allowed for the repeal of such measures and permits the leasing of the Boundary Waters land for the exploration and development of minerals and geothermal energy. A vote in any direction on the matter would need to come with great thought and consideration of our nation’s energy sector, physical environment, and public health. Ultimately, for reasons listed above, my ballot was cast against the legislation. Seeing as the matter of the Boundary Waters continues to evolve legislatively, I will be sure to remain updated and well read on the issue so that I may best represent you.
